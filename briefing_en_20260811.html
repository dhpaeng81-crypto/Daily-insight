Finance Financial Markets

US markets softened as the S&P 500 declined 0.7%, and the tech-heavy Nasdaq fell 1.1%, weighed down by semiconductor stocks like Nvidia and AMD slipping close to 3%. Global bond yields edged up, with the US 10-year Treasury yield rising to 4.2%. The Korean won stabilized around 1,370 against the US dollar, trading in a narrow range. Energy and defense sectors showed resilience amidst geopolitical risks, while a notable fund rotation from growth to traditional value stocks was observed. Safe-haven assets also gained, with the US dollar and gold rising 0.3% and 0.5% respectively.

💡 Analyst Insights
In the short term, capital is clearly shifting toward energy, defense, and wide-moat value stocks — those stable companies with strong competitive advantages. In Korea, Samsung Heavy Industries and Hanwha Aerospace stand to benefit from increased Middle East tensions and rising global defense budgets, as new contracts are anticipated. On the global stage, Lockheed Martin is positioned to gain from the heightened defense demand linked to geopolitical concerns. Growth sectors, especially AI and semiconductors, are undergoing a technical pullback, making value stocks more attractive. Investors are advised to increase exposure to energy and defense stocks in the near term, while remaining cautious of a potential sharp drop in these sectors if diplomatic resolutions ease the Strait of Hormuz crisis.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

Cybersecurity concerns around AI have escalated, highlighted by recent hacking incidents involving major AI frameworks developed by Meta, Anthropic, and OpenAI. These vulnerabilities have raised data poisoning and trust issues, triggering increased volatility in related stocks. Meanwhile, sizable investments continue flowing into AI semiconductor startups, exemplified by a US hedge fund's $400 million infusion. South Korean IT firms specializing in AI security and semiconductor material ecosystems are gaining investor attention. Longer term, the AI market is expected to grow at an annual rate of approximately 25%, although short-term risks around data integrity and regulatory oversight persist.

Energy Energy · Industry

The blockade of the Strait of Hormuz triggered a sharp 5% jump in international crude oil prices, pushing Brent crude to $86 per barrel within a single day. Energy and defense stocks in the US and Europe rallied by 3-4%, with electricity and gas futures also climbing around 2%. Meanwhile, regulatory risks heightened after a US oil company moved drilling equipment in Greenland without proper authorization. Renewable energy sectors saw modest gains driven by their strategic role as alternative energy sources, though the overall intensity of the rally remains subdued due to ongoing concerns about a global energy crunch.
