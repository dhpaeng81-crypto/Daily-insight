Nvidia Halts AI Chip Exports to Southeast Asia, Boosting Samsung Electronics & SK Hynix

AI chip export restrictions in Southeast Asia drive strong demand for Korean memory semiconductors.

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

The KOSPI surged to 2,580 points, climbing 1.2% as foreign investors showed strong net buying. The IT, semiconductor, and secondary battery sectors outperformed with gains averaging 2.3%, while the financial sector saw a modest 0.4% decline. In the U.S., the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index jumped 2.1%, lifted by optimism over tightening AI chip export regulations. European and emerging markets remained flat, supported by stable currency and commodity prices, creating a generally favorable investment climate across asset classes.

💡 Analyst Insights
Semiconductors and advanced materials like secondary batteries are poised for short-term strength. Samsung Electronics benefits from fixed semiconductor pricing gains, and SK Hynix leads in high-bandwidth memory demand amid Southeast Asia’s AI chip supply constraints, supporting strong earnings momentum. Globally, Nvidia faces direct export curbs, yet Micron is emerging as a key beneficiary in the memory market. We recommend increasing semiconductor exposure by at least 10% during this window. However, investors should remain cautious about potential expansion of U.S.-China and U.S.-Southeast Asia export restrictions, as well as supply chain vulnerabilities affecting Korean IT firms.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

Amid intensified U.S. sanctions, Nvidia has suspended server chip shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia, reshaping the AI semiconductor supply chain. The global AI chip market is forecasted to grow 15.7% this year, reaching approximately KRW 100 trillion (~USD 75 billion), with peak demand for memory and storage solutions. Domestic Korean memory makers and server manufacturers stand to gain significantly in the short term. Additionally, AI accelerator and data center investments by gaming and cloud companies will further stimulate this ecosystem.

Nvidia Halts AI Chip Shipments to Southeast Asia Amid U.S. Export Controls
Nvidia has instructed manufacturing partners to pause server chip shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia as part of the U.S.'s intensified effort to restrict AI chip exports to China. This development underscores escalating geopolitical risks affecting supply chains in Asia’s semiconductor hub. Korean memory semiconductor companies like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix stand to gain from increased regional demand amid Nvidia's export curtailment. Investors should monitor ongoing U.S. regulatory policies closely, which will influence Asian semiconductor supply dynamics and corporate earnings.

💡 Analyst Insights
The shift of AI semiconductor hubs away from Southeast Asia is driving surging domestic demand for high-performance memory and server chips. Samsung is expanding supply of HBM3, while SK Hynix leads next-generation memory market share, setting up strong earnings growth over the next 3-6 months. Globally, Nvidia faces short-term headwinds but Micron's memory business is gaining investor attention as a major beneficiary. We advise increasing Korean memory semiconductor allocations by 10-15%. Risks include possible adverse effects on Korean tech firms’ sales in China and Nvidia’s potential credit rating downgrade, which could pressure valuations.
Energy Energy · Industry

International oil prices rose 1.5%, breaching USD 77 per barrel amid supply concerns and new LNG projects in India and Argentina. The UK continues to face electricity supply warnings for a third consecutive day, with renewable energy shortfalls contributing to potential tariff hikes. India's LNG trucking expansion and significant Latin American LNG investments are expected to drive an 8% rise in global natural gas demand in 2024. Energy price volatility is anticipated to increase through the first half of the year.
