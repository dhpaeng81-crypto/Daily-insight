Bitcoin ETF Inflows Surpass $517M, Spotlight on Korean Blue-Chip Stocks

Surge in Bitcoin ETF inflows fuels Korean finance and IT rally; energy sector lags.

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

Yesterday, Bitcoin spot ETFs attracted new inflows of $517 million (approximately 691 billion KRW), boosting the Nasdaq by 0.9%. The tech-heavy index was propelled by semiconductor giants such as NVIDIA, while energy and materials sectors declined about 1% due to falling crude oil prices and weaker demand forecasts. Korea’s KOSPI rose 0.5%, driven by foreign buying and robust AI demand, with large IT and fintech stocks leading gains. Globally, easing U.S. inflation and expectations of rate cuts temporarily improved investor risk appetite.

💡 Analyst Insights
The recent surge in Bitcoin ETF inflows is a near-term catalyst benefiting financial and IT sectors. Korean fintech and financial platform firms like Kakao Pay and Woori Financial Group stand to gain from increased crypto investment activity, improving trading volumes and profitability—thus warranting overweight allocation. Internationally, Coinbase is a direct beneficiary of heightened crypto ETF demand, making it an appealing buy. While short-term corrections are possible, a further 15% rise in Bitcoin prices (above 120 million KRW) could accelerate inflows. However, heightened SEC regulatory scrutiny and geopolitical tensions such as renewed US-China trade disputes pose significant downside volatility risks.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

NVIDIA’s latest research highlights that AI agent performance hinges more on fine-tuning 'harnesses' at the application level rather than on the base AI models themselves. The AI training data market is projected to grow over 50% annually in 2024, benefiting Korean AI data and semiconductor firms. Innovations like large-scale and space-based data centers continue to drive IT investment trends. Micro1, a Korean AI data startup, recently announced $500 million in sales, underscoring sector growth. However, U.S. Department of Justice investigations into venture capital activities are casting uncertainties on funding and collaborative efforts.

Energy Energy · Industry

WTI crude oil prices fell 1.7%, closing at $78.3 per barrel amid Canadian oil production cutbacks affecting unrefined oil supply. Additional strains include supply disruptions in Latin America (notably Cuba) and energy efficiency investments in the U.S. steel industry. In Korea, price adjustments in petroleum products alongside volatile wholesale electricity prices continue, but renewable energy expansion promises long-term profitability improvements. Seasonal risks such as power shortages and cooling demand remain near-term challenges.
