AI Infrastructure Investment Halts, Volatility Heightens in Big Tech and Korean Semiconductors

AI spending fatigue signals tech correction; energy sector benefits from Middle East tensions remain strong.

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

The KOSPI declined 0.7% to hover around 2,620, led by sell-offs in technology and semiconductor shares. The Nasdaq Composite in the US fell 1.2%, driven by broad drops in AI-related stocks. Global bond yields edged up, with the US 10-year Treasury yield rising slightly to 4.55%. Geopolitical risks in the Middle East fueled gains in energy sectors. Within Korea, only the secondary battery and energy sectors showed modest gains, while other large-cap tech names underperformed relative to the broader market.

💡 Analyst Insights
Energy and refining sectors in Korea are currently benefiting from heightened global supply risks tied to Russia and Middle Eastern tensions. Leading Korean companies such as S-Oil and Korea Gas Corporation are expected to see strong buying interest due to widening margins. Meanwhile, volatility in major semiconductor firms like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix is increasing amid AI spending concerns and sector corrections. Globally, energy majors like ExxonMobil and Chevron report marked improvements in profitability, indicating a sustained uptrend in the sector. Investors should consider increasing exposure to energy stocks while adopting a cautious, wait-and-see approach on IT and semiconductor equities. However, an easing of Middle East tensions or a sharp drop in crude oil prices could trigger rapid corrections in energy shares, posing downside risks.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

The global AI and IT markets are showing signs of overheating, with excessive infrastructure investments causing investor fatigue. Intel’s stock plummeted nearly 15%, erasing approximately 12 trillion KRW in market value due to weak PC demand and overinvestment concerns. Credit rating agencies are signaling potential downgrades for AI startups like Anthropic, reflecting growing skepticism. The Korean IT industry is likely entering a 1-2 month adjustment phase following a period of rapid growth, mirroring global trends.

💡 Analyst Insights
Overinvestment in AI servers and data center infrastructure has resulted in short-term volatility, especially impacting semiconductor and equipment stocks in Korea. Companies like SK Hynix, specializing in high-bandwidth memory, and Naver, focusing on cloud and AI services, maintain solid medium-term growth prospects, though short-term caution is warranted. Globally, Microsoft stands out as a leader deploying massive AI infrastructure investments. The recommended strategy is to hold Korean IT and semiconductor stocks in the near term while selectively increasing weight in AI and cloud services sectors. Key risks include diminished AI project profitability, reductions in new AI capital expenditure, and intensified regulatory scrutiny, particularly in the US.
Energy Energy · Industry

International oil prices remain on an upward trajectory, with Brent 2026 futures surpassing $90 per barrel. Major refiners worldwide reported record quarterly profits totaling nearly 56 trillion KRW, buoyed by supply-side disruptions such as Russian refinery damage and ongoing Middle Eastern instability. This sustained supply-demand imbalance is expected to keep global energy prices elevated for the foreseeable future. In Korea, electricity and refining costs face increasing upward pressure, suggesting near-term price hikes are likely.

💡 Analyst Insights
The surge in energy prices is creating a positive earnings environment for Korean refining and retail companies like S-Oil and SK Innovation. Conversely, cost-sensitive sectors such as airlines, exemplified by Korean Air, are facing profitability headwinds. Major global beneficiaries include Chevron and BP, which posted historic second-quarter profits exceeding $50 billion. An investment approach favoring increased allocations to energy and refining stocks, alongside active buying of oil-related ETFs, is advised. Nonetheless, easing geopolitical tensions, weakening Chinese demand, or intensified government price controls remain notable downside risks to energy valuations.
