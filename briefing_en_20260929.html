AI Chip M&A, Rising Oil Prices Boost SK Hynix & Samsung Engineering

Surging AI chip deals and higher crude prices call for short-term buying in Korean semiconductors and plant sectors.

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

Global equity markets closed lower by around 0.7% with both technology and value stocks retreating amid rising US Treasury yields and higher crude oil prices. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.48% (+0.09 percentage points), pressuring growth stocks. Defensive sectors like energy and defense outperformed, gaining approximately 1.2%, while emerging markets and secondary battery-related shares declined by about 1.0%. Capital flows indicate a short-term shift into safe havens and sector rotation.

💡 Analyst Insights
The simultaneous rise in oil and yields benefits commodity-linked industries such as energy, plant engineering, and shipbuilding. In Korea, Samsung Engineering recently secured a significant $18 billion (2.3 trillion KRW) Saudi contract, positioning it as a key beneficiary. Korean shipbuilder Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering surpassed $8 billion (10 trillion KRW) in marine plant orders this year, further highlighting sector strength. Globally, Westinghouse Air Brake (WAB) recently signed over $700 million in rail service contracts, projecting profit expansion. We recommend increasing portfolio weights in energy and plant sectors while maintaining a cautious stance on growth and secondary battery stocks. However, potential easing of US-Iran tensions or a 10%+ crude price correction within three months could present headwinds. Currency fluctuations and geopolitical risks warrant close monitoring.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

The AI and semiconductor sectors are experiencing an unprecedented boom fueled by large-scale M&A and robust global investments. AMD recently announced an $8.2 billion acquisition of World Labs, founded by AI pioneer Fei-Fei Li, signaling intensified competitive positioning in AI hardware. Modal Labs, an AI infrastructure startup, tripled its valuation to $15.75 billion (~21 trillion KRW) within four months after raising about $750 million. This surge supports the broader ecosystem of data centers, AI chips, and power infrastructure, spurring substantial growth momentum in Korean semiconductor and AI ventures.

💡 Analyst Insights
In the current phase of AI chip and data center expansion, Korean semiconductor suppliers specializing in high-bandwidth memory and GPU servers stand out as primary winners. SK Hynix, commanding over 60% global market share in high-bandwidth memory and benefiting from soaring AI server demand, is a core beneficiary. Naver is reinforcing its cloud and AI platform capabilities, enhancing its competitive edge. Internationally, AMD’s acquisition of World Labs further consolidates its AI hardware dominance. We maintain a 'buy' rating on semiconductor and AI-related stocks for both short and medium term, but investors should watch for regulatory risks in the US and Europe and potential setbacks in AI semiconductor R&D which could negatively affect valuations.
Energy Energy · Industry

Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East have lifted worldwide crude oil prices by approximately 3%, with Brent crude now trading near $87 per barrel, marking a short-term peak. Despite preparations for tariff negotiations between the US and China, Iran’s hardline stance continues to inject uncertainty into global spot markets. In North America, policies aimed at securing energy and mineral resources, including LNG export strategies, are exacerbating market volatility. Domestically, import costs for power generation fuels are rising by 5-7%, increasing operating pressures on Korean industries reliant on these inputs.

💡 Analyst Insights
The uptrend in crude oil and natural gas prices directly benefits upstream suppliers such as Korean refiners and plant engineering firms. SK Innovation, which doubled operating profits last year, and SKC, expanding overseas order books, represent prime beneficiaries. Conversely, higher fuel costs impose headwinds on demand-side companies like Korean Air and Dong Suh Foods, due to increased operational expenses. Globally, integrated energy majors such as ExxonMobil continue to report robust profit growth. We advise increasing exposure to energy and plant stocks over the short and medium term, while remaining vigilant for risks related to US interest rate hikes or potential demand slowdowns driven by a Chinese economic downturn.
