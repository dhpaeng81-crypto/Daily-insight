AI Data Center Power Consumption Triples, Korean IT Stocks Face Short-Term Correction

Rapid AI growth boosts power use and regulatory risks, causing short-term market volatility

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

The KOSPI index closed at 2,780, down 0.8% from the previous day. The decline was led by weakness in U.S. AI-related tech stocks and signals of delayed Fed rate cuts. Domestic capital rotated from IT and electronics sectors, which fell an average of 1.7%, into defensive sectors like finance and construction, which gained about 0.4%. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield held firm at 4.28% amid a strong dollar. Investor sentiment is transitioning from neutral toward a cautious stance.

💡 Analyst Insights
The near-term outlook suggests continued selling pressure on Korean semiconductor and AI hardware leaders such as Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, due to heightened U.S. regulatory scrutiny and rising power costs from booming AI data centers. Nvidia faces a potential 7-10% short-term price correction amid regulatory challenges and valuation concerns globally. Investors should consider reducing exposure to IT/AI hardware stocks while increasing holdings in dividend-paying financial stocks like KB Kookmin Bank and Shinhan Financial Group, which are likely to benefit from safe-haven flows. However, a risk scenario includes an unexpected easing of U.S. regulations or strong institutional buying of beaten-down AI stocks, which could trigger a market rebound.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

The rapid expansion of AI has driven electricity consumption at over 400 U.S.-based hyperscale AI data centers to triple within the past three years. U.S. government measures to block semiconductor exports, particularly targeting Nvidia, increase compliance risks for Korean chipmakers. Domestically, heightened cybersecurity threats emerged with hacking attempts targeting Korean financial institutions. Key Korean AI stocks like Samsung Electronics, Naver, and Kakao are undergoing a short-term cooldown. The global AI market is expected to grow by 22% this year to approximately $145 billion, but regulatory and cybersecurity issues are injecting greater volatility.

💡 Analyst Insights
For Korean investors, reducing exposure to major AI/IT hardware stocks like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix is prudent due to anticipated second-quarter earnings pressure from higher data center energy costs and intensified U.S. export restrictions. Meanwhile, midstream infrastructure players such as KT and SK Broadband stand to gain from expanding data center operations. Globally, Nvidia faces a short-term downward valuation adjustment of 7-10% amid geopolitical tensions and regulatory headwinds. The recommended strategy is to trim IT/AI hardware holdings while selectively accumulating data center infrastructure stocks, all while carefully monitoring global cyberattack trends and upcoming U.S. political developments that could materially impact sector volatility.
Energy Energy · Industry

International crude oil prices rose 2.5% in three days to $81 per barrel, supported by disruptions near the UK’s Kent coast, U.S. and Venezuelan supply uncertainties, and accelerated demand from AI data centers causing U.S. power consumption to triple over three years. India’s renewable energy investments are expanding at a 9% annual growth rate, contributing to diversified energy supply. North American LNG provider Cheniere Energy is attracting investor optimism amid tightening supply-demand dynamics. The surge in AI-related power demand is reshaping energy consumption patterns worldwide.

💡 Analyst Insights
The sharp increase in power consumption by AI data centers is a positive earnings catalyst for Korean power utilities such as Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) and power plant manufacturers like Doosan Enerbility. Conversely, domestic refiners like S-Oil and SK Energy face profitability risks due to rising crude prices combined with increasing industrial electricity costs. Globally, companies like Cheniere Energy are positioned for mid- to long-term strength given tight LNG markets. Investors should consider overweighting Korean electric infrastructure firms while reducing short-term exposure to refiners and petrochemical stocks. However, geopolitical risks surrounding Middle Eastern and Venezuelan oil supplies and potential domestic power tariff hikes require vigilant monitoring.
