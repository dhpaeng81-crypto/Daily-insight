AI Investment Overheated but Real Profits Concentrated in Semiconductor Giants

Polarization in AI sector deepens; shift in Korea IT and energy investment strategy needed

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

Major U.S. stock indices exhibited volatility within a 1% range amid rising crude oil prices and interest rate uncertainties. The S&P 500 Energy sector outperformed with a 2.5% gain, whereas certain technology shares showed hesitation due to waning profit growth expectations. The US dollar strengthened, and Brent crude surpassed $82 per barrel amid inflation pressures factoring into short-term rate hike expectations. Foreign capital is rotating from emerging market large caps to developed markets’ energy and materials stocks.

💡 Analyst Insights
The surge in oil prices and growing inflation expectations have propelled the energy sector to lead near-term returns. In South Korea, companies linked to oil price movements such as Korea National Oil Corporation and SK Innovation merit attention for potential upside. Globally, ExxonMobil stands out as a prime beneficiary of the rally. We recommend a tactical ‘overweight’ stance on energy-related equities in the short term, with scope for further appreciation if inflation and rates continue rising. However, risks include oil market volatility, faster-than-expected Fed tightening, and geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and Ukraine, which could trigger sudden market reversals.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

Despite hype, most companies outside major technology giants are seeing minimal financial returns from AI investments. In the U.S., only a few firms—primarily the top two semiconductor suppliers—account for 34% of AI-driven profit growth among corporate America. Numerous AI startups and new wearable devices (such as Muse, Bee, and Friend) face high failure rates exceeding 50%. In Korea, selective investment focusing on AI data centers, semiconductor manufacturers, and IT infrastructure providers is advisable to navigate this polarized landscape.

Two AI Chipmakers Account for 34% of U.S. Corporate Profit Growth
Recent data show that the top two U.S. semiconductor AI chipmakers, including Nvidia and Micron, represent 34% of all AI-related earnings growth among American corporations. This stark earnings concentration highlights the unequal distribution of AI investment returns across the market. For Korean investors, this signals the advantage of focusing on major semiconductor players like Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, which supply these dominant U.S. firms. Investors should monitor semiconductor earnings closely as a proxy for broader AI profitability and weigh down exposure to AI ventures lacking strong financials.

💡 Analyst Insights
AI investment concentration among Big Tech and semiconductor companies results in significant earnings polarization; U.S. firms Nvidia and Micron drive 34% of total profit growth. South Korean leaders Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix stand to benefit as key suppliers of high-performance memory chips to global tech giants. Nvidia remains a dominant player internationally in both short and medium term. Investors should reduce exposure to non-core AI ventures and underperforming startups, favoring an ‘overweight’ allocation to semiconductors and data center infrastructure. Key risks include potential AI growth slowdown, tightening regulations, and intensified technological competition.
Energy Energy · Industry

Global crude oil prices rose approximately 4% to reach $82 per barrel, driven by supply concerns including production issues in Norway and the Middle East, controversies around the Rosebank oilfield, declining global inventories, and policy uncertainties. This price strength is supporting gains in energy stocks and coinciding with a 2.8% weekly drop in gold prices. Inflation expectations have also contributed to rising electricity costs across Europe and Asia.

Controversy Over Rosebank Oilfield Spurs Supply Concerns
The ongoing debate surrounding the Rosebank oilfield development in the UK has escalated supply fears amid already tight global oil markets. This uncertainty, alongside production interruptions in Norway and Middle Eastern tensions, has contributed to a sharp $82 per barrel crude price increase. South Korean oil companies like Korea National Oil Corporation and S-Oil could benefit financially from these developments given their upstream-exposure. Investors should watch geopolitical and policy developments in this oilfield case, as it could impact energy sector volatility and investment returns.

💡 Analyst Insights
The rise in crude oil prices is rapidly impacting downstream industries such as refining, energy equipment manufacturing, chemicals, and shipping in Korea. Companies like Korea National Oil Corporation and S-Oil are expected to see profit improvements and balance sheet enhancements. Globally, ExxonMobil and BP are prominent beneficiaries amidst Rosebank-related supply uncertainties. A short-term ‘overweight’ on energy and related sectors is recommended, but investors should consider risks such as manufacturing and airline cost pressures from higher energy expenses. Additional risks include the potential reversal of production cuts by oil-producing countries, weakening demand, and stricter energy policies worldwide.
