💡 Analyst Insights
The steep rise in energy prices is exerting short-term cost pressure on sectors sensitive to economic cycles such as manufacturing and transportation. Consequently, investment is rotating into defensive and high-growth areas like IT and semiconductors. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are poised to benefit from robust global AI and semiconductor investments, while US peer Nvidia remains a buy for medium-term growth given its industry dominance. Conversely, energy-intensive companies like Korean Air and Daewoo Construction face profit margin contractions. We recommend increasing exposure to IT and semiconductor stocks while reducing or maintaining minimal allocation in energy, aviation, and construction sectors. However, investors should monitor geopolitical tensions escalating energy costs long term, as this could trigger broader economic downturn risks.

The expanding AI-driven market centered on large models and data handling distinctly benefits semiconductor, server, and high-performance data center providers. Samsung Electronics (memory and system LSI) and SK Hynix (HBM, AI DRAM) are set for marked earnings improvement supported by heightened demand from global AI firms. Energy Energy · Industry

European spot LNG prices surged to the highest levels of 2023, sharply increasing electricity and industrial production costs for gas-importing countries. North American natural gas prices are also elevated, rising over 25% since the start of the year. Geopolitical uncertainties involving Iran, China’s energy strategy, and the US-Canada trade disputes further complicate the supply outlook for oil and natural gas. Key European utilities face escalating cost pass-through challenges as their reliance on gas-fired power generation rises, and delays in renewable energy transitions may slow progress toward cleaner energy sources.

💡 Analyst Insights
The LNG price spike directly pressures downstream sectors including power generation, chemical industry, and city gas suppliers. Korean city gas firms such as SK Gas and Korea Gas Corporation stand to benefit from short-term cost pass-through opportunities, potentially boosting near-term earnings. Conversely, energy-intensive industrial companies like Hanwha Solutions (petrochemicals) and POSCO Holdings (steel) are expected to face heightened input cost challenges. European energy giant Shell is a notable beneficiary given expanded LNG distribution in Europe. Investors should consider increasing exposure to Korean city gas stocks while trimming positions in resource-heavy materials and heavy industries. However, should energy demand decline or governments enforce power price caps, the ability to transfer higher costs to consumers may be limited, weakening profit momentum.
