AI Privacy Concerns Weigh on Korean IT Stocks; SK Hynix & Naver Down Over 2%

AI surveillance risks and data center regulations pressure Korea's IT and AI sectors amid global tech volatility.

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

The Korean KOSPI closed down 0.5% at around 2,665 points, reflecting pressure on domestic equities. The tech-heavy KOSDAQ index fell 1.1%, with IT and AI stocks leading the decline. In the US, the Nasdaq dropped 0.8%, influenced by the Federal Reserve's steady interest rate stance and mixed big-tech earnings reports. Global bond yields edged up, with the 10-year Treasury yield rising slightly to 4.20%, prompting a modest shift towards safer assets. Market volatility in tech stocks is expected to persist as investor sentiment remains cautious.

💡 Analyst Insights
Korean IT, AI, and data center-related companies faced short-term pullbacks due to heightened regulatory concerns and growth slowdown fears. SK Hynix and Naver both declined over 2%, impacted by US data center regulatory developments and expanding AI surveillance controversies. Meanwhile, Nvidia in the US saw increased volatility amid geopolitical and regulatory uncertainties. We recommend a cautious stance: reduce exposure to IT stocks in the near term to mitigate risk. Conversely, selectively overweight sectors tied to semiconductors and automobiles where fundamentals remain strong. Key risks include sudden tightening of US data center regulations and escalating US-China tensions disrupting global IT supply chains.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

AI privacy issues have escalated regulatory risks within the IT industry, with companies like Signal implementing strict new security measures such as screenshot restrictions to address AI surveillance concerns. New data center projects in key US regions are temporarily paused amid these privacy and security worries, while incidents of healthcare software hacking highlight increasing social side effects of AI expansion. On the growth front, Korea and China are witnessing rapid industry expansion in AI actors and agents, with market sizes projected to approach KRW 10 trillion and KRW 30 trillion respectively by 2026, underscoring a strong growth trajectory despite regulatory headwinds.

💡 Analyst Insights
Heightened privacy and regulation are expected to benefit software, data center operators, and cloud security companies. Korean beneficiaries include Samsung SDS, which stands to gain from increased demand for cloud and security solutions, and Douzone Bizon, which provides protection solutions in financial and healthcare sectors. Globally, Microsoft differentiates itself through superior data center control and security technologies. From an investment perspective, we advise reducing weights in IT platforms such as Naver and Kakao and AI companies in the short term while increasing exposure to security and cloud software providers. Investors should remain alert to the risk of overly stringent regulations potentially curbing overall market growth, as seen in possible new restrictions on data center permits and AI platform governance.
Energy Energy · Industry

Asia’s per capita electricity consumption has tripled since 2000, driving increased energy demand across the region. Oil prices stabilized around $82 per barrel amid heightened volatility influenced by US energy policies and Middle East geopolitical tensions. Investment in renewable energy continues to expand, although bipartisan debates over energy legislation in the US contribute to market uncertainty. Domestic concerns over frozen electricity and gas tariffs in Korea weigh on investor sentiment within the energy sector.
