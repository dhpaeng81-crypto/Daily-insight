AI Infrastructure Growth and US Energy Policy Relaxation Drive Market Gains

Simultaneous advances in AI infrastructure and US oil regulation brighten growth outlook amid risks

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

Global markets showed modest gains today. South Korea’s KOSPI climbed 0.7% to reclaim the 2,800 level, while the Nasdaq rose 1.1%, led by strong performances in AI-related large caps. Semiconductor, AI infrastructure, and energy sectors outperformed, whereas biopharma and gaming stocks lagged. The US 10-year Treasury yield eased by 3 basis points to 4.20%, reflecting slightly improved global liquidity conditions.

💡 Analyst Insights
The day’s market leadership came from AI infrastructure and energy sectors. Korean stocks such as SK Hynix surged 38% year-to-date, supported by booming demand for high-performance memory chips, while Doosan Enerbility rose about 25% this year, benefiting from expanded advanced energy technology and power plant supply chains. Globally, Nvidia has more than doubled this year, driven by semiconductor server demand, and ExxonMobil rose alongside easing regulatory expectations and stable oil prices around $81 per barrel. We recommend increasing exposure to AI infrastructure and energy stocks in the near term. However, investors should remain cautious about potential volatility arising from the US presidential election, sudden policy shifts, global AI regulation, and geopolitical tensions including Russia-Ukraine and Middle East uncertainties.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

AI infrastructure and data center investments are expanding rapidly. US data center capital expenditure is expected to hit approximately 50 trillion KRW this year, fueling demand for AI semiconductors and advanced power grid equipment. Korean industries benefiting include semiconductor manufacturers—especially focused on server DRAM and AI-optimized NAND flash—data center construction firms, and cybersecurity providers. Meanwhile, concerns over AI chatbot safety, data privacy breaches (such as GMO’s incident in Japan), and misuse of AI automation are mounting alongside technological progress.

💡 Analyst Insights
The core technological shift revolves around AI data center infrastructure. Leading Korean beneficiaries include Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix for high-performance semiconductors; Samsung is trading on optimism around large-scale supply contracts with US and European data center operators, reflected in a 20%+ increase in semiconductor division revenues in Q1. LS ELECTRIC is another domestic winner, supplying advanced power equipment for data centers, noted for a 17% sales increase versus last year. Globally, Nvidia dominates the server accelerator market, while Microsoft is ramping cloud data center investments by 30% annually. We advise overweight positions in AI hardware suppliers but caution on regulatory risks around AI safety and potential supply chain constraints from China’s export controls on critical materials.
Energy Energy · Industry

The US government's announcement to ease methane emission regulations on oil and gas producers boosted oil prices by about 2% to the $81 per barrel range. US natural gas prices rose 1.3%, underpinned by expectations of expanded global oil supply. Indications of relaxed restrictions on Russian diesel exports further suggest short-term supply increases in the global diesel market. Korean power, petrochemical, and refining companies are positioned to benefit from lower input costs and improved operational efficiencies.

💡 Analyst Insights
Rising energy prices primarily support refining and energy equipment producers. Korean firms such as Hanwha Ocean have expanded their order backlog by about 20% year-to-date, fueled by increased LNG ship orders aligned with green energy trends. S-Oil benefits from better refining margins, with second-quarter operating margins rising above 6%. Overseas, French energy management firm Engie is anticipated to deliver strong gains from its renewable power investments. We recommend overweighting energy infrastructure and refining stocks in the near term, while remaining alert to potential market reversals triggered by geopolitical developments in Russia and the Middle East or abrupt US policy changes post-election.
