Nasdaq Hits 19-Month Low as Major AI-Related Tech Stocks Plunge

AI regulation fears and data center opposition trigger short-term IT sector weakness

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

The Nasdaq Composite Index fell 2.21%, marking its largest decline in 19 months amid broad tech sector sell-offs. Semiconductor stocks led the downturn, with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropping 3.08%. Large-cap tech giants such as Apple and Microsoft also declined by over 2%. The global financial market reacted sharply to tightening AI regulations and increasing political uncertainty around data center expansions. Meanwhile, South Korea's domestic market experienced weakness in IT and internet shares, offset slightly by defensive sectors like secondary batteries and biotechnology.

💡 Analyst Insights
Short-term momentum in the IT and semiconductor sectors is expected to decelerate due to emerging AI and data center regulatory risks. Key Korean companies such as Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix remain vulnerable to these headwinds, warranting caution for short-term adjustments. Globally, Microsoft also faces risk from regulatory uncertainties. On the defensive side, Korean secondary battery leader EcoPro BM and biopharmaceutical company Celltrion are attracting capital inflows due to their relatively stable fundamentals. The prudent investment stance is to reduce exposure to IT and semiconductor stocks while increasing short-term allocations to defensive sectors. Should regulatory progress prove slower than anticipated, a rebound in tech shares post-correction is possible, making close monitoring of exit timing essential.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

The AI and IT sectors worldwide are facing a slowdown driven by expanding data center regulations and political risks in the US. After the Nasdaq’s sharp decline, semiconductor and AI development company stocks plummeted, amid indications of similar regulatory debates spreading across Europe and Australia. While South Korea continues to cultivate emerging demand in metaverse, generative AI, and cybersecurity fields, global investment hesitancy is decelerating new business expansions. Internationally, companies like Meta, Google, and Airbnb announce enhancements in AI services but have yet to translate these into robust investment inflows.

💡 Analyst Insights
Korean companies such as SK Hynix and Naver are likely to face short-term corrections due to data center and AI regulatory risks. Market participants are advised to adopt a wait-and-see approach until clearer signs of regulatory easing emerge. Nvidia, having already dropped over 2%, remains highly exposed to external uncertainties. In contrast, cybersecurity-focussed companies like AhnLab stand to benefit from rising demand for enhanced AI-driven security solutions over the medium to long term. Investors should remain cautious, as intensified political restrictions and profitability pressures could prolong the downtrend in technology stocks.
Energy Energy · Industry

International oil prices fell to around $72 per barrel, hitting a two-month low influenced by US midterm election outcomes and easing geopolitical tensions. Despite Iran's enhanced control of the Strait of Hormuz, concerns over global oil supply disruptions have diminished, contributing to the price slide. Concurrently, Japan and Europe announced expansions in small nuclear and fuel cell renewable infrastructure, accelerating energy system innovation. South Korea's refinery and power generation sectors largely experienced declining stock performance amid this backdrop.

💡 Analyst Insights
The sharp drop in crude prices benefits export-related sectors such as Korean airlines, exemplified by Korean Air, and petrochemical firms like Hanwha Total due to lower input costs. Companies engaged in emerging clean energy, including Hyundai Heavy Industries and Doosan Enerbility, are positioned to gain from projects expanding small modular nuclear and eco-friendly energy solutions. Globally, Bloom Energy’s 900 MW onsite fuel cell power contract with NTT DATA highlights long-term growth avenues in the fuel cell market. Given ongoing pressure from fixed costs, reducing exposure to traditional refining and power generation stocks is advisable, while overweighting airlines and petrochemical segments is favored. However, investors should remain vigilant to any resurgence of Middle East geopolitical risks that could trigger a sharp oil price rebound.
