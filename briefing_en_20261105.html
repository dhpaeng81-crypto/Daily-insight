Apple Cuts AI Budget, Korean Semiconductor Stocks Slide

Big Tech AI spending slowdown triggers short-term semiconductor and IT sector correction

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

The KOSPI dropped 1.1% to fall below the 2,600 level again, marking persistent foreign net selling for the fourth consecutive day totaling approximately KRW 210 billion. The semiconductor (-2.7%) and secondary battery (-1.6%) sectors led the losses, while defensive sectors like telecommunications (+0.7%) showed modest gains. Meanwhile, the US Nasdaq closed down 0.9%, pressured by concerns over Big Tech earnings and cutbacks in AI-related expenditures. Stronger US dollar and a 13 basis points rise in the 10-year US Treasury yield to 4.47% have continued to fuel capital outflows from emerging markets including South Korea.

💡 Analyst Insights
In the near term, IT and semiconductor sectors are expected to underperform amid confirmed AI investment slowdowns. Large Korean semiconductor companies such as Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix should be approached with caution, recommending short-term reduction or underweight positions. On a global scale, similar pressure may hit companies like Micron Technology and Nvidia. Conversely, defensive sectors including Korean telecom player KT Corp and Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) may attract short-term buying interest due to their defensive earnings profiles. Investors should remain vigilant of regulatory risks as governments worldwide increase oversight in AI, which could further dampen capital expenditure and demand.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

Apple has officially announced a significant reduction in its AI development budget, notably slashing support for its engineering teams. This reflects a broader global deceleration in AI investment momentum as skepticism grows around the immediate effectiveness of AI implementations—highlighted, for example, by low adoption of AI-assisted Uber ride bookings. Additionally, opposition to data center expansions has increased following recent US midterm elections, signaling slowed infrastructure growth. Coupled with a steep 22% year-over-year decline in global smartphone shipments in Q3 2026, Korean AI semiconductor, cloud, and smart device sectors face weakening short-term revenue momentum.

💡 Analyst Insights
Domestic Korean semiconductor giants Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, as well as cloud platform leaders Naver and Kakao, are likely to experience deteriorated earnings and stock momentum due to reduced AI investment. Globally, leaders such as Apple, Nvidia, and Google will also face growth headwinds. Data center infrastructure providers like SK Broadband and KT may see a slowdown in new contracts. The recommended strategy is to reduce exposure to AI-related Korean stocks in the short term. Investors need to watch for escalating market skepticism on AI regulation and efficacy, which could precipitate additional downside risk.
Energy Energy · Industry

Post-US midterm election developments have introduced uncertainty in energy policy, notably with the rejection of guaranteed natural gas rights and the approval of clean energy funding initiatives. Brent crude oil prices declined by 1.15%, settling near $81 per barrel, while US natural gas prices fell by 2.0%. The global energy market faces increased volatility driven by renewable energy expansion and shifting electricity and gas consumption patterns. Diverse national policies promoting energy efficiency and green infrastructure adoption foreshadow varied sectoral impacts across the energy value chain.

Colorado voters reject effort to place right to natural gas in state constitution
Colorado voters have rejected a ballot measure that sought to enshrine the right to use natural gas in the state constitution, a move backed by oil and gas industry groups. The measure aimed to protect fossil fuel usage amid growing push from local governments to ban natural gas hookups in new buildings to address climate concerns. This outcome may limit the ability of the fossil fuel sector to resist regulatory changes at the municipal level, potentially impacting demand for natural gas infrastructure firms. Investors should watch for shifts in energy policy in Colorado and similar states that could influence the long-term outlook for natural gas companies and related industries.

The decline in energy prices combined with strengthened environmental policies is benefiting Korean construction and equipment firms like Samsung C&T and Hanwha Solutions, as well as renewable energy and energy storage system (ESS) manufacturers. Conversely, chemicals, power generation, and refining sectors—exemplified by S-Oil and SK Innovation—face near-term earnings pressure, prompting recommendations to reduce holdings. Due to ongoing geopolitical risks in regions such as the Middle East and Russia and demand uncertainties, investors should pursue strategic diversification to mitigate volatility.
