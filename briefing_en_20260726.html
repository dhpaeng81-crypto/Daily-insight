Oil Surges Past $100 Amid US-Iran Conflict, S-Oil Shares Rally

Geopolitical tensions push crude higher, boosting energy and defense stocks in Korea and abroad.

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

Dow Jones futures dropped 1.4% this week amid escalating geopolitical tensions between the US and Iran, combined with expectations of a US interest rate hold. Mortgage rates hit a 2026 peak at 8.25%, further pressuring financial and real estate sectors. Investors adopted a cautious stance on tech ahead of Apple’s earnings, while Meta gained on a sizable $10 billion share buyback program. 💡 Analyst Insights
Energy and defense sectors emerge as clear beneficiaries from the geopolitical upheaval. In Korea, S-Oil stands out with projected Q2 operating profits of KRW 1.6 trillion, driven by soaring refining margins and crude prices. Hanwha Aerospace has seen shares surge nearly 20% recently, buoyed by increased defense exports. Globally, ExxonMobil—one of the world’s largest oil producers—shows strong correlation to oil price gains and remains a favored play. We recommend investors increase allocations to energy and defense stocks but remain vigilant for downside risks, including a rapid de-escalation of US-Iran hostilities or a UN intervention, which could send oil prices back toward $90 and prompt short-term market corrections.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

The AI sector faces multiple headwinds as investment momentum slows. The rapid increase in AI data center power demands has exposed critical infrastructure vulnerabilities, notably in Seoul where new data center power supply concerns are mounting. Key Korean IT giants like Naver and Kakao are reconsidering their capital expenditure plans amid global AI spending deceleration, which has slowed to roughly 12% year-on-year. While automation and AI-driven software development remain growth areas, near-term visibility is clouded by technical infrastructure and financing challenges.

Energy Energy · Industry

After breaking the $100 per barrel threshold (WTI +7.4%), oil markets are highly volatile amid US-Iran conflict tensions. The EU has relaxed methane regulations to prioritize energy security, intensifying concerns over global supply stability. Domestically, Korea faces mounting pressure for electricity and gas price hikes. Meanwhile, renewable energy policy momentum is slowing, with major economies like the US and Japan reassessing subsidy programs, signaling potential challenges for green transition timelines.

Rising crude prices directly benefit refining, gas, and defense sectors but impose cost burdens on chemicals and transport industries. Korean refiners S-Oil and Hyundai Oilbank are positioned to expand earnings due to higher refining margins. Hanwha Aerospace also benefits from stronger defense export prospects. We advocate tactical buying and portfolio overweighting in energy and defense, while preparing for a potential sharp correction should diplomatic negotiations ease tensions or strategic petroleum reserves be released.
