Oil Set to Reach $120, Boosting Korean Refining and Shipbuilding Sectors

Escalating Middle East tensions drive oil surge, benefiting SK Innovation, Hyundai Heavy, and Samsung Heavy

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

Over the past week, the US markets showed mixed performance: the Nasdaq rose 1.2%, led by tech stocks, while energy and defense sectors advanced about 2.8%. However, consumer discretionary shares slipped 1.1%, pressured by a disappointing Costco earnings report and the Chapter 11 bankruptcy of an affiliated beverage supplier. Inflation concerns persist alongside expectations for delayed Federal Reserve rate cuts. US 10-year Treasury yields edged up to 4.32%, reflecting slightly diminished safe-haven demand. The US dollar remained strong, pushing the Korean won to approximately 1,385 per dollar.

💡 Analyst Insights
The short-term winners are sectors connected to energy, defense, and AI infrastructure. In Korea, SK Innovation is poised to benefit from rising international crude prices improving refining margins, while Hyundai Heavy Industries expects earnings growth from increased shipbuilding orders. Globally, ExxonMobil stands out as a key beneficiary amid surging oil prices. We recommend increasing exposure to SK Innovation and Hyundai Heavy, focusing on companies less impacted by raw material cost inflation, such as defense and shipbuilding firms. However, investors should prepare for volatility stemming from prolonged US interest rates and potential geopolitical easing in the Middle East that could sharply lower oil prices.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

Accelerated investments in AI and data infrastructure continue to reshape the global tech landscape. The AI coding startup Cognition recently achieved a valuation of $48 billion, signaling robust investor confidence beyond current market leaders. French AI company MistralAI secured €3 billion in funding at a €21 billion valuation, with backing from Samsung and PSG Equity. Major US tech firms Meta and Google are intensifying development of AI agents and cloud services. Korean telecom leaders KT, SK Telecom, and LG Uplus are expanding AI-driven data center investments amid rising regulatory scrutiny and growing cybersecurity concerns following recent AI-related hacks.

💡 Analyst Insights
Korean firms specializing in semiconductor memory and data center operations are clear beneficiaries. SK Hynix expects increased demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) used in AI processing, while KT is likely to expand data center revenue streams. Microsoft remains a global AI and cloud leader, benefiting from synergistic growth in data center operations and AI services. We advise overweight positions in SK Hynix and KT in the near term to capture the AI infrastructure expansion. Nonetheless, investors should monitor potential regulatory tightening on data centers in the US and Europe, as well as emerging AI cybersecurity risks, which could impact growth trajectories.
Energy Energy · Industry

Heightened Western-Iranian conflict and output concerns have pushed Brent crude prices to $117 per barrel, with projections exceeding $120 medium-term. Electricity and natural gas prices have concurrently risen about 10% due to supply chain uncertainties. The US recently targeted three Iranian oil tankers, intensifying geopolitical risks, while Russia's Rosneft advances large-scale new oil development projects. The energy transition trend is underscored by significant investment in small modular nuclear reactors, exemplified by Korea’s Bluecore Energy raising $50 million.

SK Energy and Samsung Heavy Industries are expected to benefit from refining margin improvements and increased shipbuilding contracts, respectively.
