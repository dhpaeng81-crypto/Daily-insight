OpenAI Revenue Misses by $20B; Crude Oil Nears $110 per Barrel

AI investment cools amid energy price surge; cautious capital allocation advised

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

The Korean KOSPI index declined 0.9% to close near 2,760, pressured by broad sell-off in tech stocks. The U.S. NASDAQ also dropped 1.3%, reflecting global technology sector weakness. Energy and refining sectors outperformed, buoyed by rising crude oil prices approaching $110 per barrel. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield climbed 9 basis points to around 4.45%, signaling a flight to safer assets. The strengthening Korean won-to-dollar exchange rate above 1,380 is prompting capital outflows from the domestic equity market.

💡 Analyst Insights
Energy and defensive sectors are showing relative strength amid current market volatility. Korean companies such as Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO), facing emergency power supply concerns, and SK Innovation, benefiting from improved refining margins, are short-term beneficiaries. Internationally, ExxonMobil's earnings outlook improves with higher oil prices and refining margins. Investors should consider either maintaining a cautious stance or selectively increasing exposure to energy stocks while trimming technology sector holdings. However, geopolitical easing in the Middle East could trigger a sharp drop in oil prices, so a gradual, phased buying approach in energy names is recommended.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

Despite persistent optimism around AI market expansion, OpenAI's annual revenue fell roughly 20%, or KRW 20 trillion, short of earlier projections, signaling a market correction phase. Challenges such as increased data center energy consumption, controversies including recent layoffs of OpenAI safety researchers, and earnings uncertainties among global AI firms are dampening growth momentum. Meanwhile, Google launched the 'Gemini' AI workplace assistant, enhancing AI service capabilities. Korean AI semiconductor and power infrastructure companies are directly impacted by these shifts.

Energy Energy · Industry

International crude oil prices have surged between $96 and nearly $110 per barrel over the past month amid supply constraints such as reduced output in the Gulf of Mexico due to Hurricane Isaias and rising power and gas costs. North American energy infrastructure investments are accelerating alongside anticipated residential energy cost increases of up to 30% in the U.S. and Canada over three years. Concurrently, policies expanding renewables and nuclear energy usage are being implemented. These dynamics are escalating near-term energy price volatility with broad sector implications.
