Meta AI’s New Muse Wonder Model and Energy Prices Surge Over 6%

AI model competition heats up amid geopolitical energy shocks, capital focuses on tech and energy sectors

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

The Korean KOSPI closed up 1.3% at 2,765 points, reflecting strong investor appetite. The US Nasdaq rose 1.7% to 17,400 points, led by technology stocks. Conversely, financials and consumer discretionary sectors lagged, while energy stocks soared over 3% amid surging global commodity prices. The Korean won stabilized near 1,370 against the US dollar. However, persistent geopolitical tensions continue to maintain market uncertainty.

💡 Analyst Insights
Energy and AI-related technology stocks present near-term opportunities for investors. Samsung Electronics benefits directly from AI-driven semiconductor demand, while S-Oil gains from improving refining margins due to rising oil prices. Globally, ExxonMobil stands out with expected earnings boosts from higher crude prices. A short-term buy strategy with increased portfolio weight in energy and IT sectors is advisable. Nonetheless, a potential escalation in the Russia-Ukraine conflict poses a systemic risk that could trigger market-wide sell-offs.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

Meta unveiled its advanced AI model 'Muse Wonder,' intensifying competition among leading tech firms. Global investment in AI is accelerating at an annual growth rate of 110%, expected to surpass 100 trillion KRW (approximately $75 billion) in 2024. The AI-driven automation and data center markets are projected to grow over 40% annually within two years. Korean companies such as SK Hynix and Naver are rapidly reflecting positive earnings expectations driven by data and AI advancements, boosting market sentiment.

Energy Energy · Industry

Despite former President Trump's announcement of an 'energy ceasefire,' ongoing Russia-Ukraine conflicts drove WTI crude oil prices up 6.4% to $83 per barrel. Natural gas futures surged 4.7%, reflecting market instability. Wholesale power prices increased 3-5% across Europe and Asia, intensifying operational costs for the industrial sector. Meanwhile, renewable energy investments showed slight cooling after rapid short-term gains.
