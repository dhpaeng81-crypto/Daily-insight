Nvidia CEO Resignation Sparks AI Stocks Volatility

AI-related equities face short-term volatility following Nvidia leadership upheaval.

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

The KOSPI closed down 0.8% at 2,690 amidst global tech sector weakness. The U.S. Nasdaq dropped 1.2% intraday, led by semiconductor and AI stocks following the unexpected resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. Defensive sectors including banking and defense outperformed. Meanwhile, U.S. 10-year Treasury yields declined 11 basis points to 4.21%, signaling increased risk aversion.

💡 Analyst Insights
AI semiconductor shares will likely face near-term correction due to concerns over tech leadership and sector stability. Korean heavyweights Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix slid over 3% this week on dampened investor sentiment, despite resilient AI server demand supporting their mid- to long-term outlook. Globally, Nvidia is expected to weather a short-term pullback, while AMD and Google emerge as AI infrastructure beneficiaries. We recommend a cautious stance, with a buy-on-dip strategy for quality AI hardware and cloud growth stocks. Heightened regulatory risks or escalating U.S.-China tensions could amplify downside across the semiconductor and AI sectors.

Energy Energy · Industry

International crude oil prices approached $85 per barrel, rising 4% over the past week on supply disruptions caused by attacks on Russian refineries. Power and gas prices showed a slight upward bias amid growing demand in key markets and optimism around Canadian LNG exports. Renewable energy investments in the U.S. and Europe are up 3% for H1 2024, supported by grid modernization and the accelerating digital economy. This mixed environment keeps both traditional and renewable energy sectors under firm demand conditions.
