Global Bond Sell-Off Spurs Volatility: S&P 500 Falls 1.2%, KOSPI Down 0.8%

Bond market pressures drag stocks lower; energy and AI data center plays gain momentum

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

Global bond markets saw intensified sell-offs, pushing the US 10-year Treasury yield up by 11 basis points to 4.48%. This rise in yields contributed to a 1.2% drop in the S&P 500 and a 0.8% decline in South Korea’s benchmark KOSPI index. Risk aversion increased amid escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, weighing on financials, travel, and consumer sectors worldwide. Conversely, defense, energy, and AI-related IT sectors exhibited resilience amid this volatility. The Korean won weakened by 0.4% against the US dollar to 1,384 KRW/USD, with noticeable foreign sell-off in domestic bonds.

💡 Analyst Insights
Rising market interest rates due to bond sell-offs are pressuring economically sensitive stocks in the short term. However, industries tied to AI data center infrastructure and defense/energy sectors stand to benefit substantially. In Korea, SK Hynix is attracting attention for its role in AI-driven data center expansions, while Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) benefits from heightened energy demand and power price adjustments. Globally, SCAN Group’s partnership with Costco to expand Medicare Advantage plans underscores new growth avenues in insurance markets. Investment stance suggests overweighting Korean power and semiconductor stocks like KEPCO and SK Hynix, while adopting a cautious stance on banks and discretionary sectors. Key risks include further Middle East geopolitical escalation, additional US interest rate hikes, and persistent US dollar strength triggering foreign capital outflows.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

The AI and data center investment boom continues to accelerate globally, with forecasted AI infrastructure spending surpassing $400 billion annually by 2026. Google has launched AI-enhanced educational tools through Gemini, signaling expanding AI adoption in education and public sectors worldwide. South Korea’s IT, semiconductor, and data center real estate markets are structurally expanding, supported by surging orders for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) from companies like SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics. Strategic collaborations between Marvell and Google to develop AI chips further intensify the semiconductor supply chain reshaping. Data center investments remain concentrated in the US and Europe, while Korea’s leading IT firms solidify their positions in AI solution markets.

💡 Analyst Insights
The deepening AI sophistication and data center growth present clear opportunities for Korean semiconductor leaders Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, along with data center specialist real estate investment trusts like K-TOP REITs. Samsung’s enhanced partnership with Marvell and Google promises expanded global AI chip revenues, while SK Hynix is positioned to grow annual revenues by over 18% through dominant HBM memory supply by 2026. Internationally, Nvidia continues to set the standard in AI accelerators and GPU technologies. The recommended strategy is to overweight major Korean semiconductor and data center infrastructure stocks. Potential downside risks include stricter global technology regulations, rising AI resistance sentiment, and supply chain disruptions driven by Sino-American competition.
Energy Energy · Industry

Heightened tensions between Iran and the US have escalated concerns over Middle East oil supply disruptions, pushing Brent crude prices up approximately 3% to near $87 per barrel. Concurrently, electricity prices are rising in markets such as the US and UK, with announced hikes of 4–6%. Although renewable energy transitions are slowing, surging power demand from AI data centers and digital infrastructures is driving energy consumption higher. Both domestic and international nuclear and renewable energy firms are witnessing increased capital inflows amid these dynamics.

💡 Analyst Insights
Rising crude oil prices and power tariff increases create cost pressures for domestic consumption-driven companies but present clear beneficiaries within the Korean energy sector. Korea Electric Power (KEPCO) benefits directly from power price adjustments reflecting higher costs, while Doosan Enerbility is gaining from nuclear and data center-linked orders. Petrochemical firms like Lotte Fine Chemical face short-term earnings headwinds due to raw material inflation. On the global front, Chevron is poised to capture excess profits through its niche oil and gas production capabilities. Investment focus should tilt toward Korea Electric Power and Doosan Enerbility with an overweight bias, while reducing exposure to refining and petrochemical sectors. Investors should remain aware that easing geopolitical risks could precipitate sharp oil price corrections and demand contractions worldwide.
