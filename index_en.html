Data Center Supply Crunch Deepens, Sparking AI Chip & Power Stock Rally

Delays in data centers due to power and permits boost AI semiconductors and energy sectors short-term

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

The KOSPI rose 0.8% to reclaim the 2,800-point level, while the KOSDAQ gained 1.3%. US markets saw tech and infrastructure-related stocks lead gains; Nasdaq climbed 1.1% and Dow Jones 0.6%. The dollar weakened 0.3% amid easing monetary policy expectations, pulling down Treasury yields by 7 basis points. Foreign investors net bought KRW 400 billion, with strong institutional inflows. Semiconductor, power, and electric equipment sectors drove market leadership.

💡 Analyst Insights
A clear rotation toward power infrastructure, data centers, and semiconductors is underway. Locally, LS ELECTRIC stands out with an expected 18% sales growth in 2024 due to power infrastructure demand, while Samsung C&T benefits from expanded North American data center construction contracts. Globally, NVIDIA is anticipated to deliver an earnings surprise in Q2, fueled by AI chip demand and data center investments. We recommend increasing exposure to these sectors over the next 3 to 6 months. However, investors should remain cautious of data center construction delays, power grid expansion challenges, and regulatory risks like domestic electricity tariff freezes that may prompt selective profit-taking and gradual scaling down.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

New data center projects in North America and Europe face significant permitting delays, local opposition, and electricity shortages, slowing capacity growth to an estimated 7% annually in 2024-2025 despite booming AI demand. Investment is shifting toward AI-specific semiconductors and highly efficient power solution firms within limited infrastructure environments. In Korea, companies like SK Hynix and Hanwha Systems are benefiting from increasing demand for AI semiconductor components and server parts, reflecting a broader tech sector fund inflow.

💡 Analyst Insights
Data center supply bottlenecks are enhancing demand for semiconductors, communication parts, and server efficiency technologies. SK Hynix is expanding its supply of high-bandwidth memory (HBM) to North American AI server companies, while Hanwha Systems provides advanced power management and optical communication solutions. NVIDIA remains the key global beneficiary with an expected 30% revenue growth in 2024 driven by its AI server chips monopoly. We advise accumulating these stocks over the short-to-mid term (up to 6 months). However, rising AI operational costs and potential acceleration of data center permit caps or environmental regulations could heighten sector volatility.
Energy Energy · Industry

Despite a sharp drop in crude oil prices from $117 to $91, diesel and other real-demand energy prices declined less significantly. Saudi Arabia has rapidly restored production to around 8.5 million barrels per day, close to pre-Iran conflict levels. Meanwhile, competition for power in Europe and Asia has pushed liquefied natural gas (LNG) prices up over 10%. The surge in data center electricity needs is driving short-term capital flows into power generation gas and renewable supply chains.

💡 Analyst Insights
The disconnect between falling crude oil and rising localized diesel and gas prices benefits Korean firms like Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) and Samchully. KEPCO profits from wholesale power supply and growing data center demand absorption, while Samchully sees increased city gas production and related power business revenue. Duke Energy in the US, focused on expanding power grids for data centers, represents a clear overseas opportunity. We recommend overweighting KEPCO and Samchully short-term, with potential mid-term buys if electricity tariff hikes gain traction. Geopolitical risks like Middle East conflict flare-ups and changes in domestic power policies may cause sector corrections, warranting balanced portfolio exposure.
