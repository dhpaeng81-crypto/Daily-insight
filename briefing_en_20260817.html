💡 Analyst Insights
Tech and AI infrastructure-related stocks, including Samsung Electronics and Naver, remain favorable for overweight positions both short and medium term, supported by long-term growth prospects. Bill Ackman’s portfolio expansions coupled with Nvidia’s robust earnings and easing global rate signals drive inflows into these segments. Samsung leads in high-performance semiconductor demand, and Naver benefits from cloud and search engine growth, while Nvidia’s substantial capital expenditure on AI infrastructure underpins global tech momentum. Investors are advised to maintain overweight stances but monitor geopolitical risks in the Middle East and potential U.S. interest rate hikes. Weakness in auto sector performance heightens overall market uncertainty, signaling possible sector rotation from growth to cyclical stocks later in the year.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

Investment in AI and data centers is accelerating rapidly. Nvidia plans over $3 billion (approximately 4 trillion KRW) in AI computing infrastructure, while Stripe is pursuing a $7 billion acquisition of AI gateway startup OpenRouter. This surge in big tech capital is driving a forecasted 20% year-end growth in server semiconductor supply from Korean companies like SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics. The proliferation of new data centers increases electricity demand, impacting broader industrial and energy markets.

💡 Analyst Insights
Korean semiconductor leaders SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics stand to gain significantly from increasing demand for high-bandwidth memory and AI server DRAM/SSD components, making this an opportune time to overweight these stocks medium-term. Globally, Nvidia dominates with approximately 80% share of the GPU server market, fueling growth through large-scale capacity expansion. The rising need for AI-enabled hardware spans multiple industries including finance, manufacturing, and telecommunications, restructuring related markets. However, investors should be cautious of regulatory risks related to power consumption and carbon emissions, market saturation in AI services, and potential technical setbacks. Active risk management through phased investment and monitoring AI commercialization progress is critical.
Energy Energy · Industry

Ongoing threats from Houthi attacks have pushed Saudi oil tankers into stealth mode, turning off radar and increasing volatility in crude prices. Dubai crude has risen 3.5% this week, currently around $85 per barrel. Geopolitical risks in regions including Hawaii, Iran, and Ukraine add to global supply chain uncertainties. Concurrently, renewable energy investments and new refinery projects—such as Niger’s $1.9 billion facility—reflect shifts in energy infrastructure. Rising electricity costs in Asia and Europe have surged 7-8% due to supply concerns linked to power generation disruptions.
