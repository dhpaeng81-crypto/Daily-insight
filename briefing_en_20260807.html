Oil Surges 4% on Iran Strait of Hormuz Risks, Lifting Energy & Manufacturing Stocks

Energy and heavy industry rise with oil; AI security concerns weigh on Korean IT stocks

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

KOSPI closed up 0.8% to reclaim the 2800-level, led by energy and heavy manufacturing sectors advancing over 1.6%. Foreign investors net bought Korean shares worth 250 billion KRW. The S&P 500 edged up only 0.2%, reflecting mixed global sentiment. Supported by a stronger US dollar near 1,370 KRW/USD, Korean real estate and bond markets showed slight softness. Manufacturing PMI gains in the US (+1.3%) and Europe (+1.1%) have improved economic recovery prospects.

💡 Analyst Insights
The oil price jump and rising US manufacturing investment underpin a bullish near-term outlook for energy, heavy industry, and auto sectors, with potential upside exceeding 5%. Hyundai Heavy Industries benefits from growing shipbuilding orders, while Samsung Heavy Industries stands to gain from increased demand for crude oil carriers. In the US, General Motors announced over 1 trillion KRW capital expenditure on new plants, and Ford plans an additional 700 billion KRW investment in EV batteries and future vehicles. Tesla is also expanding manufacturing capacity in Europe and North America, strengthening its competitive edge. We advise overweight positioning in these cyclical sectors for the short term. However, escalating geopolitical tensions around Iran and the Strait of Hormuz remain key risks that could increase market volatility globally.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

AI technologies, led by conversational AI like ChatGPT, continue rapid adoption with unlimited free usage now available, driving broad user engagement. Yet, major players including Meta and OpenAI face emerging cybersecurity and hacking challenges undermining trust in AI systems. The global AI market is expected to grow 15% annually to reach approximately 330 trillion KRW in 2024. In Korea, IT conglomerates alongside cloud and security providers are expected to experience short-term volatility amid these concerns.

💡 Analyst Insights
Long-term AI adoption favors data centers, security, and cloud services, where Korean firms like SK Shieldus (AI-driven security) and Naver Cloud (expanding AI services) stand to gain concretely. Nvidia continues dominating the AI semiconductor market with profit margins near 48%, reinforcing its leadership globally. Energy Energy · Industry

Geopolitical tensions escalate as Iran signals tighter control over the strategic Strait of Hormuz, combined with Ukraine's attacks on Russian oil facilities, pushing crude prices up 4% to $84 per barrel. Natural gas prices rebounded over 2%, while electricity prices edged 3% higher amid seasonal demand increases. Korean energy majors are poised for improved operating conditions supported by these higher raw material prices. Contrastingly, global players like BP are divesting renewable gas assets to realign business structures.

Independent Power Producers (IPPs) compete to sell electricity to utilities and large energy consumers, influencing overall electricity pricing. The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) plays a key role by overseeing aspects of hydroelectric, natural gas, and oil markets, which can impact supply and cost dynamics in the energy sector. Investors should watch regulatory decisions by FERC and competitive trends among IPPs, as these factors can affect electricity prices and energy company valuations. Market participants in utilities and energy infrastructure sectors may experience volatility based on these regulatory and market developments.
