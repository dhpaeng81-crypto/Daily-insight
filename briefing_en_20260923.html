Global Diesel Shortage to Persist Through 2027: S-Oil and Hyundai Heavy Industries Poised to Benefit

Ongoing diesel supply crunch supports energy and shipbuilding sectors amid geopolitical tensions.

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

The KOSPI index closed at approximately 2,810, up 0.7% from the previous day, while the KOSDAQ rebounded by 0.6%. The shipbuilding, energy, and aviation sectors outperformed with gains between 2% and 4%, contrasting with slight declines in the IT and secondary battery sectors, which dropped around 0.4%. The Korean won-dollar exchange rate traded in a narrow range near 1,375 KRW/USD amid persistent global recession concerns. Meanwhile, global equities showed strength, with the S&P 500 rising 0.8% and the Nasdaq climbing 1.1%, driven largely by technology stocks. The Korean 3-year government bond yield edged up to 3.33%.

Global Diesel Shortage Expected to Persist Through 2027 Due to Iran, Ukraine Conflicts
The diesel supply shortage driven by geopolitical disruptions in Iran and the Ukraine war is forecast to continue until 2027, underpinning sustained tightness in global energy markets. This prolonged disruption maintains favorable margin conditions for refiners, shipbuilders, and shipping operators worldwide. South Korean companies such as S-Oil and Hyundai Heavy Industries stand to benefit substantially from these dynamics through improved refinery margins and increased vessel orders. Investors should monitor geopolitical developments and crude price movements as key variables influencing sector profitability.

💡 Analyst Insights
Today's market rally was led by aviation, shipbuilding, and energy industries. Korea Aerospace Industries (KAI) benefits from increased aircraft orders by Boeing and Airbus, improving its order outlook. Hyundai Mipo Dockyard is also positioned for over KRW 7 trillion in new ship orders in 2024, driven by rising diesel-powered vessel demand. Internationally, Boeing's recent share price dip has attracted institutional buying in the U.S., signaling confidence in the sector recovery. We advise a short-term buy on aviation and shipbuilding stocks with a view to increasing exposure mid-term. However, investors should exercise caution as easing diesel shortages could trigger sharp declines in oil prices and freight rates, potentially impacting earnings in these sectors.

Energy Energy · Industry

Despite Saudi Arabia restarting a key oil pipeline, international crude prices have stabilized around $84 per barrel, showing only a modest correction of about 2% from recent highs. The global diesel shortage, exacerbated by supply disruptions from Iran and the Ukraine conflict, is expected to persist until 2027. This sustained imbalance supports improved margins across refining, shipbuilding, and shipping sectors. Energy market volatility remains due to geopolitical uncertainties, fluctuating demand and supply indicators, and increased U.S. gasoline consumption. Additionally, there is growing momentum behind environmentally friendly fuel cell and LNG infrastructure expansions.

💡 Analyst Insights
Continued diesel supply constraints are driving margin expansion for South Korean refiners and shipbuilders such as S-Oil and Hyundai Heavy Industries. S-Oil benefits from diesel comprising 35% of its product slate, enhancing its profit growth potential. Hyundai Heavy’s surge in LNG carrier and crude oil tanker orders is expected to continue through 2025. U.S. oil major Chevron stands to gain from rising global refining margins. We recommend buying S-Oil, Hyundai Heavy Industries, and Hyosung Heavy Industries for both short- and mid-term holding. However, investors should watch for potential risks from production increases by Saudi Arabia or the U.S., or any geopolitical détente that could sharply depress crude prices and refining earnings, increasing stock volatility.
