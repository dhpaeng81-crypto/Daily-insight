Prolonged diesel supply crunch boosts refining margins, favoring Korean refiners and exporters

Global Diesel Shortage Caused by Iran and Ukraine Conflicts Expected to Continue Until 2027
A leading international financial institution has forecast a global diesel supply deficit lasting at least until 2027, driven by ongoing conflicts in Iran and Ukraine and refinery capacity shortfalls. This extended supply disruption is causing refining margins to surge—especially in the US and Europe—projected to more than double next year, from $300–350 to $650–700 per ton. Korean refineries such as S-OIL and Hyundai Oilbank are positioned to benefit from their diesel-heavy refining processes and export opportunities. Investors should monitor geopolitical developments and policy decisions on strategic petroleum reserves that could alter supply trajectories.

💡 Analyst Insights
The short- to mid-term beneficiary sectors include oil refining and petroleum. In South Korea, S-OIL and Hyundai Oilbank stand out due to their diesel-focused refining capacity and expanding export profitability. Globally, ExxonMobil is positioned to gain from increased refining returns and export volume in the US market. We recommend increasing exposure to these companies to capitalize on structurally higher refining margins driven by persistent diesel shortages projected through 2027. However, investors should monitor geopolitical developments, strategic petroleum reserve releases, and potential economic slowdowns that could dampen energy demand and compress margins.

Energy Energy · Industry

International reports highlight a diesel supply deficit projected to persist through 2027, mainly due to the Iran and Ukraine conflicts and insufficient refinery capacity. Refining margins in the US and Europe are expected to at least double next year, reaching $650–700 per ton versus current $300–350 levels. Despite a 3% decline in crude prices caused by recovering transit volumes through the Strait of Hormuz, regional risks in Eastern Europe and the Middle East suggest ongoing price volatility. Saudi Arabia's construction of an east-west pipeline and discussions on diesel export restrictions signal a significant shift in global supply dynamics.

💡 Analyst Insights
The sustained high diesel prices will improve profitability beyond refiners to chemical producers and transportation sectors. Korean companies like GS Caltex, with a strong export focus, and S-OIL, equipped with advanced refining units, are well positioned. Internationally, TotalEnergies is a European refining leader to watch. We recommend strategic overweight positions in refining and transportation sectors medium-term, given these margin expansions. Nonetheless, fundamental geopolitical risk from potential war escalation and a global economic downturn that could reduce oil and transport demand remain primary downside risks.
