Middle East supply disruption boosts energy sectors; recommends trimming defensive stocks

Finance Financial Markets

Defensive sectors including consumer staples and healthcare declined slightly by 0.4%, whereas the energy sector surged 2.6% on rising crude oil prices. Investors are now focusing on the interplay between soaring oil prices and a softer dollar this week.

💡 Analyst Insights
Given tightened energy supplies, we anticipate a continued short-term rally in energy-related equities. South Korean companies such as S-Oil and Hyundai Oilbank stand to benefit from expanded crude product export channels and improving refining margins. Globally, ExxonMobil is positioned as a direct beneficiary of higher oil prices. Our recommendation is to increase exposure to energy stocks over the next two weeks while reducing holdings in defensive sectors like healthcare and consumer staples to realize gains. Tech AI · Tech Trends

The global AI market is experiencing a temporary pullback as regulatory pressures mount in the US and Europe and key industry leaders agree to slow down development pace. Despite this, the sector is projected to grow at an annual rate of 23% this year. Notably, AI leaders such as OpenAI and Anthropic have postponed planned initial public offerings, amid concerns regarding valuations. Korean semiconductor and data center firms maintain positive medium-term earnings forecasts due to sustained demand for AI-related hardware.

Energy Energy · Industry

The recent attack by the Houthi rebels on Saudi oil pipelines and effective blockage of the Strait of Hormuz disrupted approximately 18 million barrels per day, or 20% of global oil supply. This led to a sharp immediate jump in international crude prices by over 10%. Asian refining margins surged up to 12%, reflecting tighter product availability. Natural gas prices also rebounded by about 6% amid confirmed logistical disruptions. In Europe, electricity futures rose, reflecting a flight to safer assets.
