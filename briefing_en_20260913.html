Oil Surges 4%, Korean Defense & Refining Stocks Rally

Geopolitical tensions boost energy, defense, CVC sectors; tech stocks remain cautious

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

US markets showed mixed sector performance with the Dow Jones up 0.7% while Nasdaq declined 0.2%. Middle Eastern geopolitical risks triggered a strong rebound in energy and defense stocks globally, pushing these sectors up 2-4%. South Korea’s KOSPI energy sector gained over 3% intraday amid a 4% surge in Brent crude oil prices. US Treasury yields rose by roughly 3 basis points reflecting market anxiety, while the Korean won weakened against the US dollar to around 1,390 KRW per USD. Foreign investors turned slightly net sellers in South Korean equities.

💡 Analyst Insights
The immediate beneficiaries are energy (refining and petrochemicals), defense, and global value chain companies. South Korean names like S-Oil and Hanwha Aerospace attracted robust inflows, with S-Oil targeted at 130,000 KRW and Hanwha Aerospace at 180,000 KRW per share. ExxonMobil surged over 5% in the US, reflecting investor optimism in energy amid supply concerns. The recommended strategy is to overweight refining and defense stocks short term while adopting a cautious stance on growth sectors such as IT and electric vehicle batteries. Investors should be ready to quickly reduce energy and defense exposure if geopolitical tensions ease or oil prices sharply drop. Prolonged instability could adversely affect South Korea’s broader manufacturing and banking sectors.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

Leading AI developers like OpenAI and Anthropic have publicly committed to slowing development to strengthen safety measures. OpenAI has officially delayed plans for a 2024 IPO, while Anthropic warned of potential AI risks within 6-12 months, signaling a cautious industry shift. This pause impacts global and Korean tech sectors with a dampened sentiment regarding new AI investments. Meanwhile, a major US AI startup, Mecka AI, secured a $500 million funding round, highlighting intense competition over AI data and talent. Korean IT heavyweights and service providers face weaker momentum amid this recalibration.

Energy Energy · Industry

Following attacks on the Strait of Hormuz and Saudi oil pipelines, Brent crude jumped 4% to surpass $105 per barrel in a single trading day. The global oil and gas supply outlook has become highly uncertain, exacerbated by US limitations on tanker defense measures that raise the risk of further attacks. South Korea faces steep rises in energy import costs, which will ripple through power generation and transportation sectors. Renewable energy stocks continue to face short-term challenges amid market volatility.

💡 Analyst Insights
The spike in oil prices affects multiple industries, from shipping and aviation to chemical manufacturing. In South Korea, refiners like S-Oil and airlines such as Korean Air stand to benefit from improved refining margins and potential fuel surcharges, respectively. Conversely, energy-intensive companies including Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) and Lotte Chemical may face widening losses due to rising input costs. Globally, ExxonMobil and Chevron are the top beneficiaries of elevated crude prices. Investment advice recommends increasing exposure to S-Oil and Korean Air while reducing holdings in energy-intensive manufacturing and transportation stocks. If the Hormuz crisis resolves or oil-producing countries increase output, energy shares may see rapid declines. Government price stabilization policies could also limit upside.
