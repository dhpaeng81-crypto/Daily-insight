Nvidia and JD.com See Major Institutional Buying, AI and Oil Prices Surge

Global funds shift to AI, semiconductors, emerging markets, and energy sectors.

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

US markets continued their upward momentum led by semiconductors and emerging market equities. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 1.8%, Nasdaq gained 1.1%, and the S&P 500 added 0.7%. Notably, institutional investors such as Bank of America and Michael Burry are concentrating large-scale purchases in key tech and emerging market stocks. Chinese consumer and e-commerce stocks, including JD.com, experience increased foreign capital inflows. Meanwhile, South Korea's KOSPI climbed over 1%, reclaiming the 2,800 level supported by strong institutional buying.

💡 Analyst Insights
The short-term beneficiaries of global capital flows are large-cap tech and emerging market consumer internet stocks. Korean giants Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, benefiting from semiconductor industry recovery and enhanced North American institutional support—highlighted by Bank of America's upgraded buying stance—show strong upside potential. Internet platforms like Naver and Kakao are increasingly linked with East Asia e-commerce players such as China's JD.com, amplifying their growth prospects. Internationally, Nvidia (target price recently raised by Bank of America) and JD.com (accumulated by investor Michael Burry) remain primary focus points. We recommend increasing portfolio exposure to these themes but advise monitoring risks from delayed US rate cuts or renewed US-China geopolitical tensions.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

AI investment and industry scale are expanding rapidly, driving North American data center vacancy rates down to an unprecedented 1.4%. Global tech leaders—OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google—are intensifying competition in AI deployment. Google is broadening AI applications into travel and financial services, pushing the frontier of AI commercialization. In South Korea, AI demand is expanding across semiconductor manufacturing, data centers, cloud infrastructure, and robotics sectors, integrating the entire AI supply ecosystem.

💡 Analyst Insights
Korean industries benefiting most include semiconductor manufacturers and cloud/data center service providers. Samsung Electronics leads with its production of memory chips and AI-specific high bandwidth memory (HBM), while SK Hynix holds the world's largest share in HBM, essential for advanced AI workloads. Globally, Nvidia remains dominant as the number one server and AI chip supplier. Investment strategies favor increasing allocation to Korean companies like Naver, expanding AI and cloud services, and LG Electronics, focusing on robotics and AI solutions. Key risks include regulatory pressures in the US and Europe and aggressive competition from China and emerging IT startups.
Energy Energy · Industry

International oil prices surged over 4% to surpass $85 per barrel amid prolonged US-Iran conflicts and supply disruptions in Canada and Nigeria. The North American power market experiences over 15% annual growth in industrial power demand due to exploding data center requirements. Capital continues to flow into South Korean energy and power generation stocks alongside US renewable energy and utilities. Geopolitical uncertainties in energy supply chains and political tensions remain elevated.
