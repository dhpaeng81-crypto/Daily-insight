Brent Oil Surges Past $100, Energy Stocks Rally Amid Inflation Concerns

Rising crude and gas prices heighten market volatility and inflation fears globally

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

Global equity markets faced increased volatility due to sharp rises in energy prices. The Dow Jones Industrial Average declined by 0.7%, while the Nasdaq Composite dropped 1.3%, reflecting a broad market correction. European indexes also saw an average decrease of 0.8%. In South Korea, the KOSPI closed down 0.6% amid the won-dollar exchange rate surpassing 1,380 won, signaling currency pressure. The energy, materials, and defense sectors outperformed, gaining between 2% and 3%, while US Treasury 10-year yields surged to 4.6%, pushing bond prices down further.

💡 Analyst Insights
In the short term, the energy, materials, and defense sectors present compelling opportunities. South Korean companies like S-Oil are benefiting from higher refining margins with its Q2 operating profit increasing by 312%. Korea Gas Corporation also gained from increased gas prices, surpassing annual sales of 33 trillion won. Globally, ExxonMobil sees a significant boost in operating earnings tied to oil price surges. Potential risks include escalation in Iran-US tensions and persistent oil price spikes that could trigger wider market corrections.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

The AI and IT sectors are attracting renewed investor attention following Nvidia's forecast of a 70% revenue growth next year, signaling robust demand for AI-related hardware. Meanwhile, OpenAI has temporarily halted new subscriptions to its Pro service due to unexpectedly high demand. Concurrently, concerns are growing over intellectual property theft and hacking incidents involving Chinese AI firms, prompting intensified regulatory scrutiny in the US and Europe. In Korea, AI semiconductor, server, and data center industries are experiencing a surge in investments and transaction volumes, growing by 15-20%, reflecting strong domestic activity.

Energy Energy · Industry

International crude oil (WTI) prices surpassed the $100 per barrel mark, driven by escalating conflict in Iran and OPEC's commitment to maintain production cuts. Natural gas prices rose by 8%, pressuring South Korea’s wholesale power prices (SMP) upward. These developments contributed to rising borrowing costs for energy-importing countries and reignited inflation concerns for the second half of the year. While renewable energy sectors face short-term relative weakness, nuclear and defense-related industries are experiencing improved supply-demand dynamics.

💡 Analyst Insights
The spike in oil and gas prices negatively impacts energy-heavy industries like Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO), which faces increasing fuel cost burdens. Conversely, refiners such as S-Oil, utilities like Korea Gas Corporation, and nuclear energy equipment makers including Doosan Enerbility benefit through higher revenues and contract awards. On the global front, ExxonMobil continues to enjoy a surge in operating profits. The recommendation is to increase exposure to energy and defense sectors while reducing allocation to power generation, steel, and shipbuilding industries due to rising input costs. However, investors should monitor geopolitical developments between Iran and the US, as well as OPEC's production decisions and demand shifts in China and Europe, which could rapidly reverse the current oil price rally.
