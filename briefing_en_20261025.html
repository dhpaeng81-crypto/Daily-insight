AI Failures Highlight, Data Centers & Infrastructure Stocks Gain Focus

Amid AI investment shakeout, data center and infrastructure beneficiaries gain investor attention.

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

The Korean KOSPI index rose 0.6% to close at 2,734 points, marking its second consecutive week of gains. By contrast, the U.S. NASDAQ declined 0.4% to 14,950 points amidst tech stock adjustments. The 3-year U.S. Treasury yields edged up 2 basis points to 3.41%, reflecting a cautious macro backdrop. In Korea, IT and data center infrastructure stocks led sectors higher with gains in the 2% range, while biotech names such as Celltrion and health supplement firms underperformed. The global dollar index fell 0.3% to 104.2, boosting trade-exposed stocks. Overall, semiconductor, data center, and infrastructure sectors are driving a structurally bullish phase.

💡 Analyst Insights
Over the medium to long term, companies involved in data centers, energy infrastructure, semiconductors, and power grid development stand to benefit structurally. Leading Korean beneficiaries include SK Hynix, which is positioned for high growth in intelligent semiconductors, and LS ELECTRIC, expanding its supply of data center power equipment. Globally, Microsoft is a key player with massive investments in cloud and AI infrastructure, sustaining positive investor sentiment. We recommend increasing portfolio weights to 10-15% in these sectors in Q2, focusing on companies demonstrating profitability and proven AI-related growth while navigating an AI hype correction. Risks include escalating U.S.-China tech tensions and resumption of U.S. rate hikes, which could pressure global IT equities.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

Following a short-term AI hype surge, increasing project failures and rising ethical and security concerns are gaining prominence. Notable examples include Apple’s repeated delays of Siri AI enhancements and multiple withdrawals or postponements of OpenAI’s 'super app' initiatives, underscoring the need for selective investment. Nonetheless, advances like Block’s new human-like voice model illustrate ongoing technological progress. The global AI market is projected to grow at 22%+ CAGR between 2023 and 2028. Domestically, surging demand for data centers, cloud services, and AI semiconductors benefits firms like SK Hynix and LS ELECTRIC. Overseas, large cloud providers such as Microsoft and Amazon are attracting escalating investment capital driven by infrastructure expansion.

💡 Analyst Insights
Investment benefits are clear for AI and cloud infrastructure, particularly data center equipment makers. SK Hynix stands out with a 40% sales increase in high-value HBM products expected in H1 2024, signaling strong near- to mid-term growth prospects. LS ELECTRIC is expanding market share with power distribution solutions for data centers, forecasting annual revenue growth of 20%. Microsoft’s annual data center investment exceeding $40 billion increasingly supports global supply chain revenue expansion. We recommend increasing exposure in the short to medium term but advise caution given the risks of AI sector bubble bursts, potential project failures, and tightening regulations around privacy and data security.
Energy Energy · Industry

Oil prices retreated modestly, with WTI crude closing at $79.5 per barrel, down 0.8%. Natural gas prices in Asia rose approximately 7% amid Indonesia’s announced export cuts to Singapore starting October 24. Emerging controversies such as undisclosed Russian-sanctioned funding for Alaska’s LNG development are raising market uncertainties. Additionally, oil spill incidents in the Middle East are causing maritime logistics disruptions. Renewable energy sectors, including solar and wind, continue robust job growth, up 15% in 2023 compared to prior years. Korean power and gas utilities are reviewing cost reduction and grid efficiency initiatives to offset rising import prices.

💡 Analyst Insights
Growth in data centers and AI adoption is driving increased electricity demand, benefiting Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) and Korea Gas Corporation (KOGAS). KEPCO is supported by a 2.1% increase in Q1 2024 power sales and expectations of tariff hikes. KOGAS sees positive outlooks from rising natural gas prices and demand expansion. Globally, companies like Vitalik Energy and ExxonMobil are poised to gain from expanding LNG contracts and investments. We recommend overweighting KEPCO and KOGAS while maintaining vigilance on short-term volatility. Potential risks include global supply disruptions, geopolitical tensions involving Russia and the Middle East, and stricter carbon neutrality regulations which could introduce policy uncertainties.
