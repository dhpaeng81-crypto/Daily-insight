Crude Oil Surges Above $108; AI Giants Face Revenue Downgrade

Geopolitical tensions spike oil prices; AI revenue forecasts cut amid cost concerns

💹 Finance 🤖 AI · Tech ⚡ Energy 📈 Stocks
Finance Financial Markets

The KOSPI declined by 0.8% today, pressured by soaring crude oil prices (+3.2% to $108 per barrel) and weakness in U.S. tech stocks. Banking and refining sectors bucked the broader decline, gaining 1.5% and 2.1% respectively, while secondary battery and semiconductor sectors corrected by 1.3%. U.S. indices reflected mixed trends with the Dow Jones gaining 0.5% driven by energy stocks, contrasted by a 0.9% drop in the Nasdaq amid tech weakness. European markets showed mixed performance amid political uncertainties in France and expectations of stable interest rates. The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield rose 7 basis points to 4.32%, reflecting inflation and policy concerns.

💡 Analyst Insights
Recent crude oil price surges and heightened uncertainty favor energy and refining stocks in the short term. S-Oil stands out with an expected refining margin increase driving a projected 2.3 percentage point rise in Q3 operating margin. Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) benefits from peak power demand and potential short-term electricity rate adjustments. Globally, ExxonMobil, which generates over $100 billion in quarterly revenue linked to oil prices, is also a key beneficiary. Given these dynamics, overweight positions in energy and refining sectors are advisable, while growth-oriented stocks and secondary battery-related companies warrant a hold. Investors should monitor geopolitical risks, such as escalating Iran-West tensions and French political instability, which could amplify market volatility especially if accompanied by further U.S. interest rate hikes.
Tech AI · Tech Trends

OpenAI’s projected annual revenue has been sharply revised downward from 94 trillion KRW (~$70 billion) to 68 trillion KRW (~$50 billion), a 27.6% cut, prompting concerns over AI-related profitability. While AI platform valuations have surged—as exemplified by Arena which increased 47.6% in corporate value within 10 months—overall investor sentiment toward technology stocks is cooling. Despite sustained domestic demand growth for AI and cloud services in Korea, layoffs and ethical debates in the U.S. AI sector are dampening short-term momentum. Infrastructure-related IT firms, including data centers, hardware, and semiconductors, remain relatively resilient amid this environment.

💡 Analyst Insights
With investor caution on core AI growth stocks, data infrastructure and semiconductor firms are poised to outperform. SK Hynix benefits from strong demand for high-bandwidth memory used in AI servers, supporting its earnings outlook. Solus Advanced Materials, delivering next-generation materials for AI data centers, also stands out domestically. Nvidia remains a leading global player with over 70% market share in server GPUs, maintaining strong growth momentum. Investment strategy suggests increasing weighting in infrastructure and semiconductor stocks while adopting a cautious stance or reducing exposure to high-valuation AI platforms and big tech growth names. Key risks include increased AI regulatory scrutiny and earnings disappointments from major AI players like OpenAI, which could induce broader tech sell-offs.
Energy Energy · Industry

Heightened military tensions between Iran and Western countries, along with threats surrounding the Strait of Hormuz, have propelled crude oil prices up 14% within a single month, breaching $108 per barrel. Production disruptions in the U.S. Gulf of Mexico due to Hurricane Isaias have further tightened supply. This price surge negatively impacts cost-heavy sectors such as domestic and international retail, automotive, and chemical industries but underpins earnings growth for refiners, gas companies, and energy firms with long-term contracts. Elevated wholesale electricity and gas prices have triggered multiple countries to reconsider adjustments to consumer electricity tariffs.
